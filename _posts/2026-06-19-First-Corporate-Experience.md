This is the first blog of many about my experiences and insights I gained during my summer internship at Health Vectors.

## Introduction
My third year had just finished and I was at a crossroads; I had to choose between an internship versus a college-issued certification. Naturally, I chose the internship as I had no work experience in my CV and really wanted to work on something grounded in reality instead of theoretical academic work.<br/><br/>

### About the company
[Health Vectors](https://www.healthvectors.ai/) is a B2B company that provides personalized healthcare analytics solutions for individuals, delivered through diagnostic labs, hospitals, clinics, and insurance companies.

## The problem statement
When I joined as an intern, I was given a simple problem statement.

>The CEO, who is a non-technical user, has a rapidly growing client/lead base and is unable to keep up with the required deliverables for his customers.

The exact requirement gathering, design decisions and implementation were left entirely to me. This has been made possible due to the presence of generative AI which accelerated development drastically and made this internship a huge success.

This blog will talk about my entire experience with requirement gathering and deciding on a solution.

## Requirement gathering and my learnings
On the first day I had a meeting with the CEO (who I was building the product for) and the CTO (who would be overseeing and validating the entire development cycle).

Frankly, my first day was rather overwhelming as I was suddenly thrown in an environment that was nothing like the dry academic environment I was used to. This was an environment where I had to adapt and think outside the box. I was given a large number of detailed requirements and pain points by the CEO and I didn't know where to start or what to focus on. Thankfully, the CTO helped me by showing me the art of how to gather requirements. By observing him, I noticed how he condensed multiple abstract pain points into concrete requirements that could be acted on.<br><br>

This was my first major learning from this internship.
> - Learn to filter information effectively and condense it till you reach the crux of the information.
> - Use a divide and conquer strategy to split the task into smaller, achievable tasks to finally complete the entire task.

A couple of meetings later, we decided on a set of requirements but those were binned entirely as the proposed solution was not quite solving the exact pain point.

After the first idea was discarded, we were able to hit the jackpot with the second idea which solved the pain points in a feasible manner. The suggested solution was a curated and user-friendly sales dashboard for the CEO that allows him to track and monitor deliverables with clients and leads in a unified interface.

This was abrupt but a learning experience by itself bringing me to my second major learning.
> Adaptability and flexibility are key; if you don't adapt, you get left behind

Both these learnings, although straightforward in text, are nuanced and of great importance in a time where AI is able to handle most implementation tasks with ease. 

AI works on the fundamental basis of Garbage In, Garbage Out; if you (the user) cannot curate and refine the information you give the AI, the AI will also return an output of similar quality.
