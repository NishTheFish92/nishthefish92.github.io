This is a quick one about [FedVAD](https://github.com/NishTheFish92/DSC-Federated-Video-Anomaly-Detection),
a small experiment in building a video anomaly detector that keeps people's
privacy in mind.

## The problem with one big dataset

The usual way to build a video anomaly detector is to collect a huge dataset in
one place and train on it. That means every camera has to send its footage over
the internet to a central server.

People aren't always comfortable with that. Singapore runs a large camera
network, and in 2018 a plan to test facial recognition on its lamp posts raised
a lot of public concern about privacy.

## Federated learning

This is where federated learning comes in. Each camera site trains its own copy
of a shared model on its own footage, and only the model's weights are sent to a
central server, which averages them into one model and sends it back. The video
itself never leaves the site.

The averaging step is called FedAvg, and with two sites it looks like this:

```
w = (n1 / n) * w1 + (n2 / n) * w2
```

Here `w1` and `w2` are the weights each site trained, `n1` and `n2` are how many
training frames each site has, and `n` is the total. So it's just a weighted
average, and a site with more footage gets more say in the shared model. The
new `w` goes back to both sites and the whole thing repeats every round.

So the goal of the project was to see whether a federated model could do about
as well as one trained centrally on all the footage at once.

## The autoencoder

The model is a convolutional autoencoder. It compresses a frame down and then
tries to rebuild it, and it's trained only on normal footage. The difference
between the original frame and the rebuilt one is the anomaly score. The higher
the error, the harder the frame was for the model to rebuild, which means
something unusual is probably happening in it.

## Did it work?

I used two public pedestrian datasets, UCSD Ped1 and Ped2, as two simulated
camera sites. The federated model came out close to the centralised one, with a
mean frame level AUC of 0.715 against 0.730.
