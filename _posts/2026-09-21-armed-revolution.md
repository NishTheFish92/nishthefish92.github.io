This is going to be a short one. I just want to talk about how great it is that Snapdragon laptops have become a real option.

## The Mac problem

For a long time, the only laptops with undeniably amazing battery life were Macs. And that came with a catch. You had to buy into Apple's ecosystem, and not everyone wants to do that.

On the Windows side it was more or less a duopoly. Only Intel and AMD were making x86 laptop chips, and battery life just wasn't anywhere near what Macs were getting.

## Then ARM showed up

When Apple's M1 came out, we got to see what ARM chips could do. As I understand it, ARM chips are generally built to be more power efficient than their x86 counterparts, and M1 was the first one I saw put that in a laptop and do it well. Great battery life and still very good performance.

I feel like Snapdragon jumped in at the perfect time after that. I'm talking about the Snapdragon X chips here, not the older stuff. Qualcomm made ARM chips for Windows laptops, and Windows has been doing a decent job backing them up. Apps that aren't built for ARM get translated using Prism, Microsoft's translation layer. From what I've seen in reviews, Prism used to be pretty rough around the edges, but these days it's actually pretty decent. It's one of the few things Microsoft has been doing right with Windows, even if they mess up a lot of other things.

To be clear, I haven't used one of these laptops myself. This is all from reviews I've watched.

## What still needs work

I still feel there's a lot of room to grow. Gaming needs to get better on ARM. And Linux support for ARM needs to get a lot better too. I use Linux, and I'm not too fond of Windows, so I'd love it if ARM was a viable option there. From what I've heard, it's just not an out of the box experience right now. You end up with workarounds for multiple apps and a lot of drivers.

## Why I'm happy about this

Honestly, I'm not entirely sure what I wanted to say when I started this. But I think it comes down to competition. When companies are competing, they keep trying to get better so they're the more attractive option for people.

With healthy competition, everyone wins. Companies work toward a better version of themselves, and we get products that keep getting better. Snapdragon showing up is good for the whole market, and that's why I'm glad it's here. I do hope it's here to stay though, since the market share for Snapdragon on Windows laptops is still in single digits.
